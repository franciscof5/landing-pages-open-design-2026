> 6 weeks. 61K+ GitHub stars. 300+ contributors. Together, we are rewriting the design workflow. Open Design is an all-in-one product design platform built for the Agent era. What we are building is simple: freeing design from the traditional canvas and bringing it into an agent-driven, end-to-end creative workflow. In the past, design meant opening a canvas, dragging rectangles, adjusting layers, slicing assets, and exporting files. The process was slow, fragmented, and exhausting. Now, all you need is to describe your idea. Agents like Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor can understand the brief, interpret the style, and generate prototypes, slides, images, and videos. From a single thought to a previewable, editable, and deliverable design asset, the entire process is being redefined. Design is becoming a capability that every agent can call. Everyone can contribute: Skills, design systems, plugins, documentation, and product ideas. Creators, designers, developers, and even agents themselves can all take part. Our vision is to spark everyone’s curiosity for beauty. And to make great design possible for more people.

> I couldn't make it to the top 3 in my first online hackathon. I'm still really glad I participated and made it to the top 15 finalists. I went into this mostly wanting to experience what it's like to take an idea, build something under time pressure, and actually present it. I ended up learning much more than I expected. For example: One of the biggest things I realized is that having an idea is probably the easiest part. Then, - The first lesson I learned was to start with the problem, not the solution. During the From Problem to Product workshop, I realized how easy it is to get excited about an idea without questioning deeply enough whether it actually solves a meaningful problem. - The Beyond the Demo session with Feifan Zheng made me think more about what happens after something works. Building a demo is one thing. Making something reliable, useful, scalable, and worth continuing to build is a completely different challenge. - The Vibe Design workshop with Yanlai Xu showed me how much difference thoughtful design can make when you're trying to turn an idea into something people can actually understand and interact with. - Anna Skrypka 's session on why startups struggle in Japan made me realize that having a good product isn't always enough. Culture, communication, relationships, and credibility can matter just as much when you're trying to enter a new environment. - And probably the biggest lesson for me was learning how to pitch. I learned that building something and explaining why it matters are two different skills. Sessions with Omid Asgari and Clay W. helped me see pitching less as simply presenting a product and more as communicating a story and a vision clearly. I also got to learn from people with very different backgrounds, including Sujan S. , Dr. Joe, Thom Peace , Katsutoshi Tabata , Ryuju Saito , Kosuke Ai, Adnan Dewan . They gave me perspectives that I wouldn't normally get as a student just starting to explore building products. For a first online hackathon, that was a lot to take in. I came away with a much clearer understanding of what I need to improve as a builder. A big thank you to Cognisor AI , Creators Circuit , Open Design , ai& all the speakers, mentors, judges, panelists, and fellow participants who made these three days such a valuable experience.
